What works

Runners don't need "light toning workouts". They need real load: heavy lifts for low reps and explosive jumps. That's what teaches your muscles and tendons to work like a spring.

Do strength after an easy run on the same day, or on your speed workout day, but after the workout. That way hard days stay hard and easy days stay easy.
2
Don't do strength the day before a speed workout or a long run. Your legs need to show up fresh for those.
3
Missed a session? Just do the next one in the plan. No need to catch up.

The workout, 6 exercises

Go in order. Rest 2-3 minutes between sets, and between jump sets rest until you're fully recovered.

Half-squat jumps

Drop into a half squat and jump up as hard as you can
Land quietly and softly, back into the same half squat
One jump at a time, no rushing
Do these first, while your legs are fresh. Once your jumps get lower, the set is over.

Goblet squat

Dumbbell or kettlebell at your chest, elbows down
Squat deep, heels down, knees tracking over your toes
Drive up powerfully, back straight
Start with no weight: spend the first 1-2 weeks learning the move with just your body weight. Then add a kettlebell, a dumbbell or a backpack with water bottles at your chest, lowering slowly for a count of 3.

Choosing the weight

The last two reps of a set feel hard, but your form holds. If you could do 4-5 more reps, the weight is too light.

Don't train to failure. Add weight once all your sets feel smooth.

Stay injury-free

1
Technique beats weight. Master the movement first, then add the kilos.
2
Your legs will be sore in the first week, that's normal. Sharp pain in a joint or your Achilles: stop and see a doctor.
3
New to strength training? Spend the first two weeks with no weight or light weight, and add the jumps from week three.
